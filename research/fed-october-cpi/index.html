Matching hikes, same gap: the yen stays weak

November 12, 2026 · Michael Lawton · Follows our October 21 weak yen note

Summary. The October CPI report came in hot, with core at 0.3% and CPI at 0.4%, higher than expected. A stronger dollar due to inflation means a weaker yen, which goes against our call. Call #001 is at risk – the dollar strengthened proving the yen weaker, and with both central banks priced to deliver matching 25-basis-point hikes nine days apart, the rate gap ends December where it started, leaving the yen little reason to get back below 158 by December 18. I'd wait till the next report in December.

Our new call, #008: the Fed lifts the top of its target range to 4.25% or higher on December 9, widening the gap with the BOJ.

What happened

October consumer prices ran hotter than forecast, and markets now treat a December 9 Fed hike as certain.

  • CPI: On November 10, BLS reported that CPI rose 0.4% in October (seasonally adjusted), against a 0.3% forecast, after 0.6% in September. Core CPI rose 0.3% against 0.2% expected, its third straight 0.3% gain (August, September, October).
  • No annual rate this month: BLS collected no prices in October 2025 during the shutdown, so it published no 12-month change for headline or core. The last official annual rates, for September, were 3.7% headline and 2.6% core.
  • What moved: Shelter rose 0.3% and made up more than 30% of the monthly increase. Food rose 0.5%. Energy rose 0.7% after 5.2% in September, and gasoline rose 1.0% seasonally adjusted. Used cars and communication fell.
  • Waller: Hours before the print, Governor Christopher Waller said in London he would back a 25 bp hike in December even if October improved, and that 50 bp should be considered if core came in around 0.3% or higher. Core came in at 0.3%.
  • Fed pricing: By the close on November 10, CME FedWatch priced no chance of a hold: 88.8% odds of a 25 bp hike and 11.2% of 50 bp (Investing.com). A week earlier, a 25 bp hike was priced at about 72%. The 5-year Treasury yield rose 10 bp to 4.75% and the 10-year 7 bp to 4.93% on the day.
  • The yen: The dollar kept rising after the print. The yen fell three days in a row and touched 160 per dollar on November 12, its weakest since September (Bloomberg). Japan's top currency official, Atsushi Mimura, warned it was too early to forget this summer's interventions (Vantage, citing Reuters).
  • The Fed so far: The Fed hiked 25 bp to 3.75–4.00% on September 16 (12–0), its first hike since 2023. It held on October 28 by 10–2, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voting to hike. October payrolls rose 165,000 against about 65,000 expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1%. The next decision is December 9, with new projections.
  • Call #001: The yen will be stronger than 158 per dollar on December 18, graded on FRED DEXJPUS. Before the CPI release on November 10, the yen traded between about 157.9 and 158.2, right on the line. On November 12 it was near 159.6 to 160 (Macrotrends), about 1.6 to 2 yen on the wrong side.

Why it happened

  • Shelter costs rose 0.3% and accounted for more than 30% of the total monthly CPI increase.
  • Energy prices rose 0.7% (including a 1.0% rise in gasoline and 2.0% in fuel oil), acting as a delayed pass-through from earlier Middle East conflict disruptions and energy market volatility.
  • According to TheStreet's inflation coverage, about 64% of the CPI basket rose by more than 3%, showing that inflation is sticky and spread across the entire economy rather than isolated to a few sectors.

How it flows through

Following the hot October CPI print, the US–Japan policy rate gap is expected to temporarily widen to 2.75%–3.00% on December 9, before snapping back to its current 2.50%–2.75% range on December 18. Both central banks are priced to deliver matching 25-basis-point rate hikes nine days apart, creating a two-step whipsaw effect for the yen.

What would prove us wrong

What would prevent a December hike, or pull the yen back below 158 by December 18:

  • A further drop in oil prices, or a sharp sell-off in equity markets (particularly driven by concerns over limits on artificial intelligence data center construction, as well as uncertainty in capex sustainability), by December 9 (Brent settles below $80, or the Nasdaq closes 15% or more below its October record; source: ICE Brent and the Nasdaq Composite).
  • A steep drop in crude or diesel prices would ease headline and supply-chain inflationary pressures, showing up in November CPI on December 10 (the energy index falls 2% or more in November; source: BLS).
  • A deeper correction or sell-off in technology and stock markets, by December 18 (the Nasdaq closes 20% or more below its October record; source: Nasdaq Composite).

Call #001 (open): the yen is stronger than 158 per dollar on December 18, 2026, graded on FRED DEXJPUS. Our calls can't be edited after publishing, so #001 stays up and will be graded as written.

Call #008 (new): the Fed raises the top of its target range to 4.25% or higher on December 9, 2026, widening the gap over the Bank of Japan's 1.25% policy rate to at least 2.75–3.00 points. We grade it on the Fed's December 9 FOMC statement. Wrong if the Fed leaves its range at 3.75–4.00%.
