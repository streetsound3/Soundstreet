REACTION · JAPAN · CENTRAL BANKS

The BOJ held as expected. The yen should still strengthen.

November 2, 2026 · Michael Lawton · Follows our October 22 preview

Summary. This was the expected hold: the Bank of Japan kept its rate at 1.25% on October 30. I think ETF sales will matter. The yen will strengthen and I stand by open Call #001 because I think the coming inflation will help to balance out the yen.

What happened

  • The BOJ held its policy rate at 1.25% by an 8–1 vote. Board member Hajime Takata dissented and proposed raising it to 1.50%.
  • The Outlook Report says underlying inflation “is at a level that is generally consistent with the price stability target of 2 percent.” In July it said inflation was “approaching” 2%.
  • The fiscal 2027 core inflation forecast was raised to 2.6% from 2.4%, and the BOJ said inflation risks are “skewed to the upside.”
  • The BOJ will speed up sales of its ETF holdings to about ¥860 billion a year from about ¥330 billion, starting January 2027.
  • The yen strengthened after the decision, trading below the mid-157s per dollar during Asian hours.
  • This was the hawkish hold our preview called as its base case.

Why it happened

The BOJ raised its inflation forecast “and stressed anew its readiness to continue pushing up borrowing costs to forestall mounting inflation risks,” as Reuters put it. Governor Ueda said “the pace (of future rate hikes) will depend much on upcoming data.”

How it flows through

If Japan raised rates to combat inflation this could work to strengthen the yen as the rate gap between the dollar would lessen. Or Japan could divest in the dollar and invest in the yen.

I think ETF sales will matter. The BOJ’s ETF sales matter because the Bank of Japan has been one of the largest institutional shareholders in Japan’s stock market. By gradually selling its holdings, it is reducing a source of demand that has supported Japanese equities for years.

What would prove us wrong

  • The yen staying weak would prove this wrong.
  • The U.S.–Japan interest-rate differential widens again.
  • If the BOJ repeatedly delays tightening despite inflation pressures, investors may conclude that Japanese rates will remain relatively low.

Our call (Call #001) stands: the yen will be stronger than 158 yen per dollar on December 18, 2026. We grade it with the Federal Reserve’s published daily yen rate.
