REACTION · TRADE · CHINA

China extends rare earth export pause, pushing the next deadline to January

November 12, 2026 · Parker Onacilla · Follows our November 3 note

Summary. China extended its suspension of the expanded October 2025 export controls on November 9, 2026, one day before the original November 10 deadline. The extension delays the potential supply shock rather than resolving the underlying supply-chain concentration. I remain bullish on alternative producers, but less confident about an immediate catalyst.

Our call: Maintain Call #005, with lower conviction.

What happened

  • China's Ministry of Commerce and General Administration of Customs issued Announcement 58 of 2026.
  • The announcement keeps six October 2025 export-control announcements suspended from November 11, 2026, through January 10, 2027.
  • Four of the six announcements relate to rare earths, covering additional elements, processing equipment, overseas-related rare earth products, and technologies. The other two concern superhard materials and lithium batteries/graphite.
  • This means China did not allow the expanded controls to return on November 10. It extended the suspension for another two months.

Important distinction: China’s April 2025 licensing requirements for seven heavy rare earth elements, including dysprosium, terbium, and yttrium, remain in effect. The extension does not remove those restrictions. The next major deadline is now January 10, 2027, when the latest suspension expires. China has not guaranteed another extension.

What it means for our thesis

Our original thesis was bearish on rare-earth-dependent manufacturers and bullish on non-Chinese producers. The extension weakens the immediate version of that thesis because manufacturers have another two months without the expanded restrictions taking effect. This reduces the near-term likelihood of a sudden supply shock caused by those specific measures.

However, the longer-term thesis remains intact:

  • China still dominates rare earth refining and permanent magnet manufacturing.
  • Existing restrictions on seven heavy rare earth elements remain active.
  • Manufacturers continue to have an incentive to diversify suppliers and reduce dependence on China.
  • MP Materials and Lynas remain strategically positioned to benefit from increased non-Chinese production.

What stands out:

  • MP gained 6.61% on November 9, outperforming the S&P 500’s 2.35% gain, but gave back 3.42% the following day.
  • Lynas gained another 4.18% on November 10, its first full trading session after the announcement.
  • Most of the manufacturers we identified as potential losers also gained on November 9. Their November 10 performance was mixed.
  • From November 6 through November 10, MP gained approximately 2.96%, Lynas gained 11.29%, and the S&P 500 gained 1.95%.

Interpretation: The initial performance of MP and Lynas was encouraging relative to the S&P 500. However, the manufacturers did not experience a consistent sell-off. The broad market rally on November 9 and other company-specific factors make it difficult to attribute these movements directly to China’s announcement. Two trading sessions provide very limited evidence about the longer-term thesis.

Names to watch

MP Materials MP

▲ Still bullish, lower conviction

Its US-based mining, processing, and permanent magnet manufacturing operations position it to benefit from domestic supply-chain investment.

Lynas Rare Earths LYC

▲ Still bullish, lower conviction

Its established mining and processing operations outside China give manufacturers an alternative source of rare earth materials.

Governments and manufacturers continue to prioritize supply-chain diversification because of China’s dominance. The two companies have different operational risks, and increased demand does not guarantee higher profits or stock prices. Still, I expect the structural demand for non-Chinese production to support their relative performance over the next year.

What would change our mind

The next major catalyst is January 10, 2027. Four developments would change my outlook:

  • Another extension. If China extends the suspension beyond January 10, the immediate supply-chain risk decreases further. This would weaken the near-term bullish case for MP and Lynas.
  • Restrictions become less restrictive. If China eases its April 2025 licensing requirements or demonstrates consistently faster export approvals, the urgency of finding alternative suppliers would decline.
  • Supply diversification accelerates. If manufacturers successfully secure alternative suppliers, build inventories, or expand recycling, their dependence on Chinese materials could fall faster than expected.
  • MP and Lynas struggle to execute. If either company experiences production delays, rising costs, or fails to expand capacity, its ability to benefit from industry demand could be limited.

Final call: Maintain Call #005. MP Materials and Lynas should outperform the S&P 500 through November 3, 2027, based on the long-term shift toward non-Chinese rare earth supply chains. The November extension reduces near-term conviction, but I would reconsider the call if China extends the suspension again or the companies fail to translate industry demand into production and financial growth.

One important distinction: January 10 is the next policy deadline. The latest announcement does not specify exactly what happens afterward, so we should wait for China’s next official announcement rather than assume the suspended controls automatically return.
