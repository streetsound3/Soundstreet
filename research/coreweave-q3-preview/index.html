Neocloud slipping? Is CoreWeave the next in line for poor earnings?

November 10, 2026 · Michael Lawton

Summary. CoreWeave (CRWV, Nasdaq) reports third-quarter results after the close on Tuesday, November 10, having lost about half its value in a month and with its 2032 bonds yielding above 16%. Given the growing disbelief in AI and tech, along with the ongoing Nvidia investigation, CoreWeave’s earnings release will be poor. Our call (Call #007): CoreWeave closes lower on Wednesday, November 11, than on Tuesday, November 10.

What happened

  • The stock. CoreWeave fell 49.9% from Oct 6 to Nov 6; Nebius (NBIS, Nasdaq) fell 47.7% over the same days.
  • The bonds. CoreWeave’s 9.625% notes due 2032 traded at a yield above 16% on Nov 6. It sold those notes in June.
  • The debt. Borrowed debt was $35.1B at June 30, plus about $16.5B of lease liabilities. Q2 interest expense was $640M, against $128M of adjusted operating income.
  • The election. Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the Nov 3 midterms.
  • Microsoft. CNBC reported on Nov 6 that Microsoft plans to use fewer neoclouds and build more of its own data centers. It hasn’t said which providers it will drop; CoreWeave is among those named. Microsoft was 62% of CoreWeave’s 2024 revenue.
  • Nvidia. Nvidia’s board opened an independent investigation on Nov 8, after a New York Times report that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence. China denies it. The board says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised. Nvidia owns about 11.5% of CoreWeave after a $2B investment in January.
  • IREN. IREN (IREN, Nasdaq) rose about 20% on Nov 6 after its results. Its backlog grew 22% in the quarter to $20.3B.
  • Wayve. On Monday, Nov 9, CoreWeave announced a multi-year deal to train self-driving company Wayve’s AI on Nvidia Blackwell chips. Terms weren’t disclosed.
  • Dates ahead. CoreWeave reports Nov 10 after the close (call at 5 p.m. ET). Nebius reports Nov 12 before the open.

What to watch on November 10

Even at the midpoints of its own guidance, CoreWeave’s adjusted EPS comes in below the Street’s: −$1.33 against −$1.27. Interest expense runs about four times adjusted operating income.

Why it disappoints

  • Strain stays concentrated on the weakest borrower (24/7 Wall St., Nov 6).
  • “On November 6, 2026, the 2032 bond of CoreWeave (NASDAQ:CRWV) traded at a yield above 16%, even though it pays a coupon of only 9.625%. Lenders usually ask for that kind of return only when they doubt a company can keep refinancing.” (24/7 Wall St., Nov 6)
  • High borrowing costs are testing CoreWeave’s expansion plans.
  • “I do agree completely that neoclouds in particular are going to be the most impacted,” Anastasia Amoroso, chief investment strategist at Partners Group, told CNBC’s “Closing Bell” on Friday. “Unlike hyperscalers Amazon and Microsoft, the top neoclouds are unprofitable and heavily dependent on a small number of customers.” (CNBC, Nov 9)
  • Last week’s midterm elections, which produced sweeping victories for Democrats, added to uncertainty about the pace of the AI buildout.
  • “CoreWeave and Nebius have both lost about half their value in the past month as they get set for earnings reports this week.” (CNBC, Nov 9)
  • Nvidia uncertainty amid both company affairs and the ongoing investigation.

How it flows through

  • The risk in a CoreWeave earnings disappointment is that CoreWeave sits in the middle of an interconnected AI infrastructure ecosystem. Its financial results can reveal problems in several different parts of the AI investment cycle.
  • Applied Digital (APLD, Nasdaq) develops data centers and leases capacity to companies like CoreWeave.
  • Reuters reported that CoreWeave had approximately $50 billion in debt in October 2026. If CoreWeave struggles to demonstrate attractive returns on that investment, lenders may reassess the entire neocloud sector.
