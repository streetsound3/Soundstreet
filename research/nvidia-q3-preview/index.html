Nvidia’s beat streak keeps going

November 16, 2026 · James Chen and Michael Lawton · Call by James Chen

Summary. Nvidia (NVDA, Nasdaq) reports Q3 results after the close on Tuesday, Nov 17, with the stock sliding into the report and no permanent CEO. The company beat Wall Street’s revenue estimate 8 quarters in a row, and CoreWeave just showed that AI chip demand is still strong with $3.66B in revenue vs. $3.55B expected. Our call (Call #009): Nvidia’s Q3 revenue comes in above $110.2B, the top of its own guidance range.

What happened

  • The report. On Nov 17, Nvidia reports results at 4:20 p.m. ET (Nvidia).
  • Last quarter. Their Q2 revenue was $96.2B and EPS was $2.22 vs. $2.10 expected (Aug 26, Nvidia).
  • CoreWeave. CoreWeave’s Q3 earnings just beat expectations and it had a $132.5B backlog (Nov 10, CNBC).
  • Microsoft. Microsoft plans to use fewer neoclouds (Nov 6, CNBC).
  • The CEO. The board opened an independent investigation of the CEO (Nov 8, Nvidia). Jensen Huang resigned on Nov 12, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board looks at internal and outside candidates (Nov 12, Nvidia). Ian Buck, an Nvidia executive, has been reported as a possible successor; nothing is confirmed (Nov 15, Fortune).
  • Washington. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged Nvidia’s board to hire its next CEO from outside the company (Nov 15, CNBC). On Nov 16, the Commerce Department issued a rule targeting Chinese companies that rent Nvidia computing power from data centers abroad (Nov 16, CNBC).
  • HSBC. HSBC downgraded Nvidia to Hold on Nov 13 and cut its price target to $170, saying the stock struggles until there’s a permanent CEO. Nvidia closed at $162.85 that day, down about 2% (Nov 13, Yahoo Finance).
  • The stock. The stock fell nearly 8% the week before Nov 9 (Nov 9, CNBC). On Nov 16 it closed down 4.1% at about $156.14, roughly 10% below its $173.73 close on Nov 11, the last day before the CEO news. Micron and Intel fell about 7% each (Nov 16, Stock Titan; Motley Fool).

Why

  • Nvidia has had 8 consecutive revenue beats, by about 4–5% each. Last quarter had guidance of $91B and an actual of $96.2B, beating its own guidance by 5.7%; to clear $110.2B it only needs to beat it by 2%.
  • CoreWeave’s beat and $132.5B backlog show that buyers are still spending and demand remains high.
  • Though there was some bad news (Microsoft using fewer neoclouds, the board investigation, and Huang’s resignation), it all happened after Oct 25, when the quarter ended, so it can’t change Q3 revenue.

How it flows through

A beat ripples both ways. Suppliers such as TSMC, which makes Nvidia’s chips, and Micron, which makes memory, sell more. Buyers like CoreWeave look smarter for spending big. Nvidia is also one of the largest companies in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, which means a beat can lift the whole market.

What would prove us wrong

  • A weak outlook. Nvidia’s Q4 revenue outlook comes in below the Street’s $124.95B. Graded Nov 17 from Nvidia’s Q3 release.
  • Margins slip. Gross margin comes in below 73.5%, the bottom of Nvidia’s guidance. Graded Nov 17 from the Q3 release.

We track this as Call #009: Nvidia’s Q3 FY27 revenue comes in above $110.2B, the top of its own guidance range. The number is reported revenue, graded on Nov 17 from Nvidia’s Q3 press release at 4:20 p.m. ET. Revenue of $110.2B or less means we were wrong. We’re calling the revenue number, not the stock’s reaction: Nvidia could top $110.2B and still fall if the Q4 outlook disappoints or there’s no news on the CEO search.
