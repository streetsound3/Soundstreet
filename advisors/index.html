The call after the headline

A Fed decision, a currency slide or a market drop hits the news, and clients call asking one question: what does this mean for me?

Clients expect market updates, quarterly letters and a clear answer on the phone. Most small firms have no one whose job is to write them, so the advisor writes them late at night, or doesn't.

We write the first draft, so you can spend the time with your clients.

Read the full advisor edition →

You may have seen headlines about the Japanese yen, which is trading near its weakest level against the dollar in decades. The main reason is simple: interest rates in the US remain much higher than in Japan, which pulls money toward the dollar. For investors, this mostly affects how international holdings translate back into dollars. It can make Japanese stock returns look lower for US investors than the headlines suggest, even when the underlying companies are doing well. Many large Japanese companies actually benefit from a weak yen because they sell their products around the world. We don't see this as a reason to change a long-term, diversified plan, but we're watching it closely and are happy to talk through how it affects your portfolio.
